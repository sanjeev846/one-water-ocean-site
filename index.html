Newsletter

This Ocean Planet

A Media for Change newsletter on ocean preservation and stewardship, issue by issue.

This Ocean Planet publishes regular dispatches that mix hard marine science with the people living closest to it — an issue on how blue holes form and why scientists study them, a piece on the SOFAR channel and how whales and fish use sound to communicate across entire ocean basins, a profile of a shark diver working to change how people see the animals she photographs, and dispatches from youth-led efforts like coastal mangrove cleanups.

New issues go out to readers worldwide, written and reported in large part by young storytellers in the OCEAN community.

  • Format Substack newsletter
  • Publisher Media for Change
  • Topics Marine science, conservation, youth-led stories
  • Status Publishing regularly

Immersive / VR

Dean's Blue Hole VR

The world's deepest known blue hole, 663 feet down — no scuba tank required.

Dean's Blue Hole, off the coast of Long Island, Bahamas, is a vertical descent into some of the ocean's least-seen light and pressure. This VR experience puts young viewers inside that descent alongside free-divers and marine biologists, pairing the physical sensation of dropping through the water column with real science on how blue holes form, what lives in them, and why they matter to ocean research.

The project is being built by a small team of student storytellers and VR developers within the OCEAN community, working with mentors who've filmed and dived the hole in person.

  • Format 360° VR experience
  • Subject Free-diving, marine biology, blue hole geology
  • Location Long Island, Bahamas
  • Status In production

Book series

The Ocean's Call

Hand-carved linocut prints bring ocean creatures — and the threats they face — into classrooms.

Each book in the series pairs an original printmaking piece with a story about a single ocean creature, written for young readers but grounded in real marine science. The bold black-and-white linocut style is designed to hold up on a classroom wall as easily as on a page, and each story folds in a conservation issue the creature actually faces — habitat loss, overfishing, plastic pollution — without losing the sense of wonder that pulled the reader in.

Storytellers and student printmakers in the OCEAN community contribute original art and text, with mentor feedback from working illustrators and marine educators.

  • Format Illustrated book series
  • Medium Original linocut printmaking
  • Audience Classrooms and young readers
  • Status Ongoing, new titles in development

Education

Ocean College

A peer-learning cohort where storytelling and marine science sit at the same table.

Ocean College brings together a small cohort of young people to study ocean systems — currents, coral, fisheries, climate — directly alongside working scientists and storytellers. Rather than a lecture series, it runs as a working group: cohort members bring a story idea or research question, then spend the term developing it with input from peers and mentors, ending with a project they can carry into their own OCEAN pitch.

Sessions run virtually so students anywhere in the world can take part, with periodic live meetups tied to the broader OCEAN community calendar.

  • Format Peer-learning cohort
  • Length One term, virtual sessions
  • Who it's for Students and young storytellers
  • Status Enrolling for the next cohort

Documentary Film

Silfra

One drop of glacial meltwater, a decades-long journey beneath Iceland.

SILFRA follows a single drop of water from the Langjökull glacier down through Iceland's volcanic bedrock to the Silfra fissure — the crystal-clear crack between the North American and Eurasian tectonic plates, famous for water so pure divers can see nearly 100 meters. Along the way, the film uses that one drop as a throughline to explain how glaciers, groundwater, volcanoes, and plate tectonics are all part of the same connected system.

The film is guided by geologists and dive scientists who work in Silfra itself, and is being developed as a documentary short with an eye toward festival and classroom distribution.

  • Format Documentary short film
  • Subject Glaciology, plate tectonics, freshwater systems
  • Location Þingvellir, Iceland
  • Status In development

Film + Graphic Novel

Sagarmatha

Three generations, one road trip, toward the mountain the world calls Everest.

When octogenarian journalist Sundar Saha loses the newspaper job he held his entire working life, he sets off with his daughter Pradipta and his influencer granddaughter Noyoni on a road trip to Nepal — toward Sagarmatha, "the universe is not someplace else," as the mountain is known there long before it was named Everest. The story is as much about the three generations' different relationships to attention, work, and awe as it is about the mountain itself.

Releasing as a paired short film and graphic novel, Sagarmatha connects a very ocean-and-water-focused community to the mountain systems that feed it — glacial melt, river systems, and the connectedness of water from peak to sea.

  • Format Short film + graphic novel
  • Setting Nepal, en route to Sagarmatha / Everest
  • Themes Family, journalism, nature and disconnection
  • Release 2027

Origins

Twenty-five years of water storytelling

2000

Founded at the University of Miami

The One Water project begins as a research and production initiative at UM's School of Communication, examining global water systems through documentary film.

2008

One Water — the documentary

Narrated by Martin Sheen, the feature documentary premieres after three years of field work across 14 countries. It wins Best Documentary at the Foyle and Columbus International Film Festivals. Watch on the original One Water site → · Read on Wikipedia →

2009–2012

387 million households

Discovery Networks broadcasts One Water internationally, reaching an estimated 387 million households and becoming a foundational text for the global water justice movement.

2013

Media for Change founded

Building on the documentary's reach, Media for Change launches as a global 501(c)(3) nonprofit dedicated to empowering purposeful storytellers to drive social change.

2016

Stockholm World Water Week

One Water is presented at Stockholm World Water Week, connecting with the global water security policy community and expanding the project's international network.

2025

2,300 schools across India

A water-focused storytelling curriculum reaches more than 2,300 schools, demonstrating the reach of media-as-education at national scale.
